# Methodology and limitations

This tracker is an independently maintained record of publicly reported contract disputes between U.S. healthcare providers and insurers. It is designed to make a fragmented phenomenon easier to inspect, not to claim comprehensive surveillance of every negotiation in the country.

## Inclusion

A record represents a publicly reported contracting dispute involving a healthcare provider organization and an insurer where network participation, termination, negotiation, or patient access is materially at issue. Each published record includes a primary source URL.

## Dates and status

- `first_media_date` is the earliest public reporting identified during research. It is not necessarily the date negotiations began.
- Deadline, out-of-network, and resolution dates are recorded when supported by public sources.
- Status reflects the latest reviewed public evidence. An old “ongoing” status may mean no later resolution was found, not that negotiations are certainly still active.

## Patient impact

Patient figures may be reported directly or estimated from available enrollment, market, or provider information. The dataset distinguishes estimated figures through its source and method fields. Aggregates that mix reported and estimated values should be interpreted as directional rather than exact.

## Coverage bias

The tracker is visibility-biased. Large systems, large insurers, disputes affecting many patients, and markets with active local reporting are more likely to appear. Absence from the dataset does not mean a dispute did not occur.

## Review and corrections

Automated discovery may identify candidate records, but it does not publish them directly. Additions and status changes require human review. Corrections, missing cases, and better sources are welcome through the repository issue or pull-request process. Accepted changes are preserved in version history and release notes.

## Reuse and citation

The repository release will specify a data license before public launch. Until then, cite the project name, release date, repository URL, and the underlying sources for individual records.
